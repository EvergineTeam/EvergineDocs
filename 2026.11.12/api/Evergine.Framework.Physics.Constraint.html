    Remarks

    A constraint lives on the entity of the body it constrains. The other side is either the body held by ConnectedEntity, or the world itself when no entity is selected, which is how a hinge is pinned to a wall that is not simulated.

    Anchors are given in the local space of their entity and converted to world space when the constraint is created, using the poses the bodies have at that moment. That is why a constraint has to be created after the bodies are in place: it captures the relationship it has to keep.

    Jolt does not break constraints on its own. When IsBreakable is set, the manager reads the impulse the solver applied after every step and turns it into a force, which is what BreakForce and BreakTorque are compared against.

    IsHolding

    Gets or sets a value indicating whether this constraint is holding its bodies.

    Declaration
    [RenderProperty(Tooltip = "Whether the joint is holding. Off lets the bodies come apart without moving the anchors.")]
public bool IsHolding { get; set; }
    Property Value
    Type Description
    bool
    Remarks

    Switching it off lets the bodies come apart as if the joint were not there, while the joint itself stays where it is and remembers what it was holding. Switching it back on takes hold again from the original anchors, which is worth bearing in mind: a joint switched on after its bodies have drifted apart will pull them back together.

    This is not the same as disabling the component. Disabling the component removes the joint from the simulation altogether and re-enabling it builds a new one from wherever the bodies have got to, so the anchors move. This keeps the joint and its anchors and only stops it acting. Nor is it the same as breaking it, which is permanent and raises Broken.

    Recreate()

    Rebuilds the constraint, which is needed after changing anything captured when it was created.

    Declaration
    public void Recreate()
    Remarks

    The old constraint is destroyed here and now rather than queued for removal. Queuing both halves would cancel them out, since enqueuing an add drops the constraint from the removal queue, and it also has to happen while the bodies are still alive: this is what a body about to be destroyed calls so the solver is not left holding a constraint that points at freed memory. The add stays deferred, and retries on its own while the bodies it needs are out of the world.
